Some businesses do not have a traffic problem. They have a vocabulary problem.

Their service solves something real and expensive. The people who need it are out there, with budgets, right now. And the keyword tool returns zero, because those people have never heard the name of the service and would not type it if their project depended on it.

Owners in this situation usually draw the same conclusion: our niche does not work online. It is a reasonable conclusion from the evidence they have, and it is wrong.

This is how one of those niches was turned around, what the numbers actually prove, and what I would check first on a site like that.

## Two years of a live website and not a single lead

The company does 3D laser scanning and BIM modelling for buildings, infrastructure and landscapes — [the rebuild and the figures are in the case study](/portfolio/architecture-website-rebuild-and-seo-scan). The work is genuinely valuable: it produces measured drawings and models of things that exist but were never documented, or were documented wrongly decades ago.

The website existed. It was built on a no-code builder, it was slow and it was unstructured, and for two years it produced nothing. Not few leads — none.

The niche made it worse. By the company's own estimate, over ninety per cent of its potential clients did not know the service existed at all. Not «had not chosen a supplier yet» — did not know that a building can be measured this way.

That is the hard version of this problem. You are not competing for attention. You are not in the consideration set, because there is no consideration happening.

## Why a service with no search volume still gets found

Here is the thing that makes this solvable.

Your buyer does not search for your service. But he is searching for something, right now, and it is connected to the moment when he will need you.

He has been told to produce a document he does not have. He has inherited a building with no drawings. He has a machine whose manufacturer no longer exists. He has a deadline from somebody else and a gap he cannot fill.

He types that. Not your technology — his problem, in his own words, in his own industry's vocabulary.

Demand for an unknown service is never zero. It is displaced: it sits in the words for the problem, the document and the deadline, and the keyword tool shows it clearly, as long as you stop asking it about yourself.

## The mistake that keeps these sites invisible: ranking for the name of the technology

Almost every site in an unknown niche is built the same way. The home page announces the technology. The service pages describe the equipment. The portfolio shows point clouds.

It reads as expertise. It ranks for nothing.

«3D laser scanning» is searched by competitors, students and journalists. Occasionally by an engineer who already uses it and already has a supplier. The person who will actually pay you does not know that phrase exists.

The page he needs is not about scanning at all. It is about a floor plan of an existing building, an as-built record for a handover, a deviation check before a fit-out. Scanning is how you do it, and «how» belongs in paragraph four, not in the H1.

This is the single change that moves these sites: stop describing the method, start naming the outcome the buyer can already picture.

## Where to find the words your buyer actually types

When the tool returns zero for your service, it is answering a different question than the one you asked. Four places hold the real answer.

### The document somebody demanded from him

Almost no technical purchase starts with desire. It starts with a requirement.

A bank, an authority, a main contractor, an insurer or a landlord asks for a document, and the person who was asked has to find a way to produce it. He searches for the document by its exact official name, because that is the word that was used in the email he received.

Make a page per document. Name it the way the demand letter names it. That is a buyer with a deadline, which is the best kind.

### The industry he works in, not the one you work in

You are in scanning. He is in facility management, or heritage conservation, or shipbuilding, or food processing.

He will never find a generic service page, because he is not looking for a service — he is looking for someone who has done it in his kind of building, with his constraints, around his equipment. One page per industry, written in that industry's vocabulary, finds people a general page cannot.

### The contractor he already hired

The unknown service almost always sits next to a known one.

Before anyone needs a 3D scan, an architect, a surveyor or a general contractor is already on the project. The buyer searches for that neighbour, and pages built around the neighbour's process catch him on the way.

### Your own phone log

The cheapest research in this trade costs nothing: read what people actually wrote and said when they contacted you.

Those are the words of people who found you despite your site rather than because of it. The phrasing in those emails is the phrasing to put on the pages.

## Programmatic SEO done honestly: why three hundred landing pages is a map, not spam

That rebuild ended with more than three hundred landing pages, and that number makes people uncomfortable, because it sounds like the bulk pages everyone has learned to distrust.

The difference is what each page is for.

A spam page is one template with a city name swapped in, built to exist. A map page is one real pair — this industry, this deliverable, this region — with its own buyer, its own objection and its own first question.

If you cannot say who arrives on a page and what they were told to produce, the page should not exist. If you can, that page is often the only route by which that person will ever discover the service at all.

Three hundred pages is not a strategy. Three hundred answered questions is.

## What the rebuild changed, and what the numbers actually prove

The site was rebuilt from scratch on clean code, the design the company liked was kept, loading time went under one second, the structure was rebuilt around services, industries and regions, three languages were added and schema markup was put in place.

The results, in the company's own measurement: organic traffic up twenty-nine times within months, top three in Poland and top twenty in the United States for niche keywords, visibility across several markets, and inbound leads every week where there had been none for two years.

Now the honest part, because this is where case studies usually lie.

Twenty-nine times sounds enormous and is partly an artefact of the starting point. When traffic begins at almost nothing, any real improvement produces a large multiple. A site with decent traffic does not get a 29× from the same work.

Several things also changed at once — speed, structure, language coverage, three hundred pages — so the multiple is the sum of all of them, not proof that any single lever did it.

What the case does prove is narrower and more useful: in a niche where the owner had concluded that online does not work, a site built around the buyer's vocabulary produced weekly enquiries from zero. The claim is about the direction, not about your own future multiple.

## AI assistants are the discovery channel for a service nobody searches for

This matters more here than in an ordinary niche, and it is new enough that most competitors have not noticed.

A person who does not know your service exists cannot search for it, but he can describe his problem to an assistant. «I have a warehouse and no drawings, how do I get accurate plans» is a question people now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity or Gemini before they ever open a search engine.

The assistant answers by naming a method. Then, often, by naming companies.

That is discovery happening on your behalf, and whether you are in that answer depends on things you control: readable text instead of PDFs and galleries, numbers worth quoting — tolerance, turnaround, price ranges — a page that explains the method in plain words, and the same company details everywhere you appear.

### Answer engine optimization (AEO) when there is no search demand to optimize for

Generative engine optimization and answer engine optimization sound like new disciplines, and in most niches they are a refinement. Here they are the main road.

When nobody types the name of your service, the assistant is not an extra channel on top of search — it is the only place where the buyer's problem gets translated into your method. That makes the ordinary AEO work disproportionately valuable: explain the method in plain words, publish the figures somebody could quote, and keep your company details identical everywhere.

In practice, for a niche like this, it is the same work that makes the site legible to a human who has never heard of you.

### What to mark up on a service page so an assistant can quote you

A language model reads your text, but markup is what tells it which parts are facts. Without it the page is prose it has to interpret. With it the page carries named entities: this is a service, this is who provides it, this is what it costs, these are the questions it answers.

In practice three things cover almost everything. Mark up the service itself — what it produces, which areas you serve, what a project costs. Mark up the questions you really get asked, as a proper FAQ rather than a styled accordion. And mark up the company, with the name, address and phone identical everywhere you appear, because that is what lets a model decide that four mentions are one firm.

This matters more in an unknown niche than in a crowded one. A well-known brand is recognised anyway: the model has seen it discussed a thousand times. Nobody discusses your service, so you never accumulate those signals — and markup plus identical details is the cheapest substitute for being famous.

## A one-hour check for an owner in a niche like this

Open your own site and answer honestly.

Does the H1 on your main service page name the technology or the result? If it names the technology, you are invisible to everyone outside your trade.

Is there a page for each document your clients are asked to produce? Named the way the demand letter names it?

Is there a page for each industry you have actually worked in, in that industry's words?

Can someone get a price range, a turnaround and a tolerance without writing to you?

Is there anything on the site a language model could quote — a number, a method, a limit — or is it adjectives and photographs?

If the answer is no five times, the problem was never the niche.

## Frequently asked questions

**Our keyword tool shows zero for everything. Does that mean SEO is pointless for us?**
It means the tool was asked the wrong question. Zero for the name of the service is normal in these niches; the demand sits in the words for the problem, the document and the deadline. Those have volume, and they are usually far less competitive than the obvious terms.

**How long does this take before leads appear?**
First movement at about six weeks, meaningful growth on a three to six month horizon. In niches like this the pages that carry a document name or an industry move fastest, because the intent behind them is immediate.

**Do we have to publish prices?**
A range, with a line on what moves it. Almost nobody in these niches does, and almost every enquiry begins by asking. It brings the buyer who was going to ask anyway and keeps away the one with no budget.

**Is three hundred pages realistic for a small company?**
Not as a starting point, and not as a goal in itself. Start with five: the two documents clients ask for most, the two industries you have the best references in, and one page that explains the method in a language an outsider understands.

**Will AI assistants send us real clients?**
They already send the question. In a niche where the buyer does not know the service exists, an assistant describing the method and naming suppliers is often the first time he learns there is a method at all.

**Is this just programmatic SEO under another name?**
The mechanics overlap: many pages generated from a structure. The difference is the structure. Programmatic SEO gets its bad name from templates filled with a city list; here every page is one real pair of industry and deliverable, and the test is whether you can name the person who lands on it and the document they were asked to produce. If you cannot, delete the page.

**We are a small team with no marketing person. Where do we start?**
With the phone. Write down the exact words of the last twenty enquiries, including the ones you turned down. That list is your keyword research, and it costs nothing.
