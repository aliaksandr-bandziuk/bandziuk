## Why a property developer website has to sell something the buyer cannot see yet

A developer sells a building that does not exist. There is nothing to walk through, nothing to touch, and often nothing on site but a fence and a board. Everything the buyer uses to decide has to be carried by the website, which makes it a different instrument from an agency site: not a catalogue of what is available, but the substitute for a physical object.

That changes what belongs on it. Floor plans and renders are the easy part. What decides the inquiry is the material that makes an unbuilt property feel real and safe — construction progress with dates, the permits and the legal structure of the purchase, the payment schedule, and who exactly is behind the company.

### What a buyer of an off-plan unit checks on a developer website

Someone buying before completion is managing risk, not choosing a kitchen layout. They look for what has already been delivered, whether the previous projects were finished on time, whether progress is documented rather than promised, and whether the terms of the deal are written down before a call.

A site that answers those questions loses fewer buyers at the point where they hesitate. A site that shows only renders leaves the question open, and an open question at that price point usually ends the visit.

### Why a developer website needs traffic before the launch, not after

Search visibility takes months to build. A developer's selling window is fixed by the construction schedule, so a site that starts earning organic traffic at launch has already missed the part of the cycle where inquiries matter most. The work has to begin while the project is still in permits — the pages, the structure and the search visibility built ahead of the sales phase, not alongside it.

[Renovation and investment website with a premium feel and 96/98/100/100 performance](/portfolio/renovation-and-investment-website-with-premium-design) is a case study from the same market, where speed and trust signals were treated as part of the sales instrument rather than as a finishing touch.

## Real estate developer website examples: what the best ones show

Lists of the best real estate developer websites are dominated by 3D tours and video headers. The parts that actually help a buyer decide are simpler, and they work for a developer of any size:

- a page for every development with the location, completion date, unit types and prices or price ranges

- an availability table that is kept current — sold units marked as sold, not removed

- floor plans that open properly on a phone, with areas in square metres

- construction progress updates with dated photos

- one enquiry path per development, sent to the people selling it

A real estate developer website template can present one project well. It rarely copes with several developments, each with its own units and sales status, without turning into pages nobody keeps up to date.

If you build for clients rather than sell units in your own developments, see the [construction company website](/construction-company-website) page.
