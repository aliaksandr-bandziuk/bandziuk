## Why a real estate agency website loses to the property portals it competes with

An agency website almost always starts as a catalogue. Listings go up, filters get added, and the site is considered done. The problem is that a catalogue is the one thing an agency will never win on. Portals hold more inventory, more traffic and larger search budgets, and a buyer looking only for listings goes there first. A site that competes on inventory competes on the single axis where it is guaranteed to lose.

What the agency does own is everything a portal cannot show: which districts you actually work in, which segment you know, which language your buyers speak, and what happens after the first inquiry. That is the material the site should be built from.

### What a buyer checks on a real estate agency website before making contact

By the time someone reaches your site they have usually seen the property somewhere else. The visit is not a search — it is a check. They are deciding whether to send the inquiry to you or to the agency open in the next tab. They look for how long you have worked this market, whether the listings are current, whether a named person answers, and whether terms are stated or withheld until a call.

None of that is decoration. It is the content that decides between an inquiry and a closed tab, and it is exactly what a catalogue with a contact form at the bottom leaves out.

### Why multilingual work matters more for a real estate agency than for most businesses

Property is one of the few categories where the buyer routinely sits in a different country from the asset. A single-language site quietly filters out everyone searching in another language, and the agency never finds out — those buyers simply never appear in the statistics.

Building the second and third language properly is not translation. It means separate keyword research per language, content adapted to how that market reads, and a URL structure search engines can tell apart. [Build and optimize a multilingual real estate platform](/portfolio/build-and-optimize-a-multilingual-real-estate-platform) is the case study for exactly that work.

A site for one agent rather than an agency — personal brand, neighborhood pages, IDX listings — is covered on the [realtor website design](/realtor-website-design) page.
