[MAIN CONTENT]

### What a real estate website in Cyprus needs: international buyers, five languages, luxury demand

A buyer of a villa in Cyprus is rarely in Cyprus. They are in Munich, Zurich, London, Warsaw or Tel Aviv, they search in their own language, and they take months to decide. So the website does the work a sales office would do: it has to be found, it has to answer questions before anyone asks them, and it has to show facts a buyer can check.

When I started in early 2025, there was nothing to inherit — no domain history, no traffic, no content. That made it possible to build SEO and AI search into the platform itself instead of adding them later. It also meant that every visitor the site has today was earned, because nothing was bought.

### Building the property platform from scratch: catalogue, map and project pages

I designed and built the platform myself. The first version ran on Next.js with Sanity as the CMS and was hosted on Vercel. Leads went straight to the company's CRM, and bulk import scripts made managing projects and articles 16 times faster.

The core is the catalogue: projects in Paphos, Limassol and Larnaca with filters by city, property type, price and bedrooms, and the same catalogue on a map. Every project has its own page with a gallery, the price, the covered area and plot size, the energy class, distances to the beach, schools, hospitals and the airport, and a PDF brochure. Developers' pages group their projects.

Every public page is rendered on the server, so search engines and AI crawlers read the full text without running any scripts — the filters, the sliders and the map included.

### SEO without bought links: how the site grew in Google from zero

Real estate is a niche where buying links is routine. On this project it was ruled out on principle. Not a single link was bought. Every external link came on its own or from directories where I registered the company myself.

What grew the traffic instead was the site itself:

- pages for each way buyers search: city, property type, stage of construction, budget, beachfront, luxury villas over a million
- landing pages whose project lists are built from the live catalogue, so they never go stale
- project pages with facts that match the searches: price, area, bedrooms, location
- a blog that answers the questions buyers have before, during and after a purchase

The result: from zero to about 2,500 organic visits a month. Search brings 96% of all traffic. There is no Google Ads budget at all.

### German-language SEO for buyers from Germany, Austria and Switzerland

The DACH market is the site's main audience, so the German version is not a translation of the English one. German buyers search with their own words, ask their own questions and compare Cyprus with places they know. Articles such as where Germans live in Cyprus were written for them from the start.

The German version now holds top positions for many queries. English, Polish, Russian and Hebrew follow the same rule: each language has its own keyword research and its own angle. A Polish or German buyer is an EU citizen and buys under different rules than a buyer from the UK or Israel, so the articles explain the purchase for the reader's own situation.

All five versions are linked with hreflang and an x-default, the Hebrew one laid out right to left. Each buyer lands on the page in their language.

### GEO: how the property website gets into ChatGPT and AI answers

Buyers increasingly ask ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews before they ask an agent: is Paphos or Limassol better for a family, how much money do I need beyond the price, can I buy remotely. Getting into those answers is GEO and AEO — generative engine optimization and answer engine optimization.

For this site that means:

- facts stated as text on every page: prices, areas, locations, completion dates, distances
- structured data describing the business and the questions buyers ask
- comparison articles that answer the exact questions people put to assistants, with a clear conclusion
- the same facts in every language, so an assistant does not find two versions of the truth

About 10% of organic visits now come from AI assistants. They are tracked as a separate channel in GA4, next to Google and Bing.

### Content that answers buyers' questions: comparison articles and localised guides

The blog has around 80 articles in each of four languages. They were planned from Search Console data and from the gaps in the buyer's journey, not from a list of nice topics. The early articles clustered around "should I buy" and "which country or city", while buyers also need answers about money, ownership and selling later.

A separate cluster compares options head to head: Paphos or Limassol, Cyprus or Greece, Cyprus or Turkey, a new build or a resale, an apartment or a villa. These are the questions people ask before they choose, and the questions AI assistants are asked most often.

### Moving to a VPS and a custom admin in 2026 without losing addresses

In June 2026 I moved the whole platform from Sanity and Vercel to a VPS with a custom admin panel and a new data pipeline: projects and units now come from developers' feeds and are updated every day, so prices and availability on the site stay current without manual work.

Every old address got a 301 redirect to its new one, and the sitemaps were split by page type: projects, developments, developers, landing pages, the blog and case studies. Together they hold about 2,670 addresses, and each one is meant to be indexed.

### Technical SEO fixes that brought pages back to Google

Running a site of this size means finding problems before they cost traffic. A few that I found and fixed:

- sold-out projects without a price crashed their pages, and 52 pages across four languages returned a server error and told Google not to index them
- pages left over from a design preview still carried a noindex tag and were invisible in search
- a robots.txt rule hid the site's styles and scripts from Google, so it saw pages without their layout

None of these was visible to a visitor. All of them were visible to Google.

### When lifestyle copy replaced property facts: how commercial pages lost traffic and came back

While I was on leave, descriptions on the project cards were rewritten without an SEO review. Facts about price, area and location gave way to lifestyle copy about sunsets and the sea.

It read well, but it changed who came to the site. The pages started to attract informational searches, and the strongest commercial pages — the ones people find when they are ready to buy — dropped out of the results and stopped getting visits.

When I came back, I rewrote the descriptions on every project card with the facts buyers search for: price, area, bedrooms, location, completion. Clicks grew by about 16% with almost the same impressions, and the commercial pages are now returning to their positions and their traffic.

In spring 2026 there was another, external dip: uncertainty in the region hit demand and traffic. The site came through it without changing course and without turning on paid ads.

### Results of SEO and GEO for the Cyprus real estate website in numbers

- about 2,500 organic visits a month, starting from zero in early 2025
- 96% of all traffic from search, no Google Ads
- about 10% of organic visits from AI assistants
- top positions for many German queries in the DACH market
- not a single bought link
- a steady flow of enquiries in a segment where demand is never mass demand

I built this site and I still run it: development, content, technical SEO, AI search and analytics are done by one person. That is why problems are found in days and fixed in the code, not passed between teams.

### What this case shows about SEO for a property business

A property site does not need bought links to grow. It needs pages for the way buyers actually search, facts that search engines and AI assistants can quote, a version for each market rather than a translation, and someone who notices when something breaks. The same approach works for a developer, an agency or any business that sells something expensive to people who take their time.
